SAAI Code of Conduct
1. Our commitment
SAAI is a community for developing and sharing Semantic and AI-enabled Information Architecture knowledge, ontologies, patterns, methods, documentation, software-related artefacts, and practical experience.
We are committed to creating a professional, open, welcoming, and intellectually honest environment in which people can contribute across differences in discipline, organisation, role, experience, language, culture, and perspective.
Participation in SAAI is based on good faith, mutual respect, curiosity, transparency, and a shared responsibility for the quality and health of the community.
2. Expected behaviour
Participants are expected to:
Treat other people with respect, patience, and consideration.
Welcome questions, critique, and alternative perspectives offered in good faith.
Distinguish clearly between evidence, interpretation, assumptions, and proposals.
Give and receive constructive feedback with attention to both substance and tone.
Challenge ideas, models, and decisions without attacking individuals or groups.
Acknowledge uncertainty, mistakes, dependencies, and limitations.
Credit the work and contributions of others appropriately.
Respect applicable licences, attribution requirements, confidentiality obligations, and intellectual-property rights.
Protect personal, confidential, security-sensitive, and organisation-specific information.
Use shared repositories, issues, discussions, meetings, and other community spaces for their intended purpose.
Seek clarification before assuming harmful intent, while remaining accountable for the effect of one's actions.
Support participation by people with different levels of experience and different professional or academic backgrounds.
Disclose relevant conflicts of interest when contributing to decisions or recommendations.
Help repair misunderstandings or harm when they occur.
3. Unacceptable behaviour
The following behaviour is not acceptable within SAAI community spaces:
Harassment, intimidation, threats, or persistent unwelcome personal attention.
Discrimination, stereotyping, or demeaning treatment of individuals or groups.
Insults, ridicule, personal attacks, trolling, or deliberately inflammatory communication.
Repeated disruption of discussions, meetings, reviews, or collaborative work.
Deliberate misrepresentation of another person's contribution, position, or evidence.
Knowingly presenting speculation as established fact.
Plagiarism or failure to provide required attribution.
Publishing or sharing private, confidential, or identifying information without appropriate authority.
Retaliation against a person who raises a concern or participates in addressing one.
Abuse of a maintainer, moderator, reviewer, steering-group, or repository role.
Any conduct that a reasonable professional community would regard as unsafe, coercive, exclusionary, or seriously disruptive.
4. Scope
This Code of Conduct applies to all SAAI community spaces, including:
GitHub repositories, issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, and project boards.
Community meetings, workshops, courses, conferences, and working groups.
Mailing lists, chat spaces, collaboration platforms, and social-media accounts operated for SAAI.
Public activities where a person is formally representing SAAI or acting under an SAAI mandate.
This Code does not prevent respectful disagreement, rigorous review, or firm governance decisions. It requires that such activities remain professional, proportionate, transparent, and focused on the work and the health of the community.
5. Responsibilities and authority
The SAAI Steering Group is accountable for maintaining this Code of Conduct and for establishing a fair enforcement process.
The Steering Group may delegate operational responsibilities to designated community moderators or maintainers. Delegated responsibilities and decision rights should be documented and made visible to the community.
People responsible for enforcement are expected to:
Act impartially and in good faith.
Protect the privacy and safety of people who report concerns.
Avoid conflicts of interest and step aside when impartiality may reasonably be questioned.
Gather sufficient information before deciding on an action.
Use proportionate responses and document significant decisions.
Communicate decisions and reasons to affected parties when appropriate and lawful.
Maintain records with access limited to those who need them.
6. Reporting a concern
Concerns may be reported privately to:
henri@saai.community
A report should include, where possible:
A description of what happened.
The relevant date, place, repository, issue, meeting, or communication channel.
Links, screenshots, or other supporting information, where appropriate.
Whether there is an immediate safety, privacy, or confidentiality concern.
The reporter's preferred method of contact.
Anonymous reports may be considered, although anonymity can limit the community's ability to investigate or respond. Reports will be handled as confidentially as reasonably possible. Information will be shared only when needed to assess the concern, protect participants, comply with applicable obligations, or implement a decision.
No person should be disadvantaged for raising a concern in good faith.
7. Response and enforcement
Depending on the nature, seriousness, frequency, and impact of the conduct, a response may include:
Informal guidance: A private clarification of expectations and requested change in behaviour.
Formal warning: A documented notice identifying the concern, expected corrective action, and consequences of repetition.
Contribution moderation: Editing, rejecting, reverting, locking, or removing content or contributions that do not meet community standards.
Temporary restriction: Time-limited restriction from specified community spaces, roles, or activities.
Removal of responsibility: Withdrawal of maintainer, moderator, reviewer, working-group, or other delegated responsibilities.
Community exclusion: Removal from SAAI community spaces or activities in serious or repeated cases.
Referral: Escalation to a hosting organisation, platform operator, employer, event organiser, or competent authority when required or appropriate.
Actions should be proportionate, consistently applied, and focused on protecting the community, correcting behaviour, and repairing harm where possible.
8. Review and appeal
A person directly affected by an enforcement decision may request a review by the SAAI Steering Group, unless the Steering Group made the original decision collectively.
Where the Steering Group made the original decision, it should appoint an impartial review group or identify another documented review mechanism. Anyone with a conflict of interest must not participate in the review.
A review may assess whether:
The process was fair.
Relevant information was considered.
The decision was proportionate.
The Code was applied consistently.
The review outcome should be documented and communicated to the affected person, subject to privacy and confidentiality constraints.
9. Relationship to other rules
This Code of Conduct supplements, and does not replace, applicable law, contractual obligations, institutional policies, event rules, platform terms, or professional duties.
Where another applicable rule sets a higher standard or requires a specific response, that rule takes precedence for the relevant context.
10. Maintenance of this Code
The SAAI Steering Group should review this Code periodically and when significant governance, community, or platform changes occur.
Material changes should be proposed transparently, with an opportunity for community input appropriate to the significance of the change. The repository history serves as the authoritative record of revisions.
